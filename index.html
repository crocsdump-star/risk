BIG IDEA

Risk management in Tourism and Hospitality Management is the methodical process of identifying, assessing, controlling, and monitoring risks that may affect guests, employees, facilities, business operations, and the environment. It involves direct interaction with people, food, accommodations, transportation, and public facilities (structural and super-structural) — and is essential in maintaining a SAFE, SECURE, and HEALTHY ENVIRONMENT.

WHY IT MATTERS

1–5

Protects guests & employees · Prevents accidents, illnesses, property damage · Maintains cleanliness & public health · Reduces financial loss & disruption · Ensures compliance with laws & standards

6–8

Builds customer trust & satisfaction · Protects the establishment's reputation · Ensures business operational continuity during emergencies

Golden Rule

"Prevention is better than cure" — anticipate hazards and act before they cause harm to people, the environment, or the business.

Core Definitions

🛡️ Safety

Focuses on preventing accidents, injuries, and emergencies.

🔒 Security

Protects guests, employees, property, information, and business assets from threats such as theft, violence, unauthorized access, fraud, and other security incidents.

🧼 Sanitation

Focuses on maintaining cleanliness and preventing the spread of disease/contamination — proper food handling, personal hygiene, cleaning & disinfection, waste disposal, pest control, and sanitary guest rooms/public areas.

    Emergency Procedures — The 10 Components

    Emergency Procedures are planned and organized actions that must be followed when an unexpected event threatens the safety, security, health, or property of guests and employees.

      SECURITY CONCEPTS

      Threat

      Any person, condition, activity, event, or situation that has the potential to cause injury, illness, property damage, financial loss, or disruption of operations.

      Vulnerability

      A weakness or condition that makes a person, facility, system, or organization more likely to be affected by a threat.

      After: Coordination with Authorities, Post-Emergency Evaluation.

      Risk Assessment Techniques

      7 COMMON TECHNIQUES
      1. Identify the hazard situations, places, and things
      2. Assess the Risk Matrix (e.g., severity vs. likelihood)
      3. Inspection and walkthrough examination
      4. Job Safety Analysis
      5. Standard checklists
      6. Analysis of incidents and accidents
      7. The "what if" analysis
      7-STEP RISK ASSESSMENT PROCESS
      1. Determine the context of the scenario
      2. Identify the potential hazards
      3. Analyze the risk and conclude its gravity (severity)
      4. Evaluate and prioritize what is acceptable vs. non-acceptable to standards
      5. Select control measures to use as procedures
      6. Implement the control practices
      7. Monitor and review effectiveness and adjust as needed
      🧠 Memory Tip "It's Always Important Jobs Stay Analyzed Wisely"
      = Identify hazards → Assess Risk Matrix → Inspection/walkthrough → Job Safety Analysis → Standard checklists → Analysis of incidents → What-if analysis.

      Risk Treatment & Communication

      Risk Treatment refers to the actions taken once a risk is identified. Options fall under five categories:

      Avoidance

      Eliminate the activity or exposure that creates the risk entirely.

      Reduction

      Lower the likelihood or severity of the risk through controls.

      Sharing

      Transfer or distribute the risk (e.g., insurance, contracts, partners).

      Retention

      Accept the risk when it's within tolerable limits.

      Escalation

      Refer the risk to a higher level of authority for decision.

      CRITICAL THINKING FRAMEWORK

      The central question in risk treatment is: "What should we do about the risk?" Communication must then answer:

      • Who needs to know?
      • What do they need to know?
      • How should we tell them?

      Answering these prevents incidents and protects people, the environment, places, and property. Communication must be accurate, timely, and understandable for people affected internally or externally.

      🧠 Memory Tip "A Rich Squirrel Really Escapes"
      = Avoidance → Reduction → Sharing → Retention → Escalation. For communication, just remember 3 W's: Who, What, and how to tell them (the third "how" completes the trio).

      Case Study: The Busy Lunch Service

      During a peak lunch hour at a hotel restaurant, a server accidentally spills a pitcher of water on the main walkway between the kitchen and dining area. Because of the rush, no staff member cleans it up or places a caution sign. Simultaneously, a kitchen staff member props open the rear emergency exit door with a box to cool down the hot kitchen — leaving the back entry unlocked and unattended.

      PRACTICE — think it through, then reveal

      1. Identify the primary Safety hazard and the primary Security vulnerability.

      Safety hazard: the unattended water spill on the main walkway — a slip/trip hazard during peak service.

      Security vulnerability: the propped-open, unlocked, unattended rear emergency exit — allowing unauthorized access into the establishment.

      2. What immediate actions should management or staff take?

      Immediately clean/dry the spill and place a "wet floor" caution sign to prevent slips; assign or remind staff of the standing rule that emergency exits must stay closed except during actual emergencies, and have security/a designated staff member close and monitor the door. Both corrective actions should be logged as part of incident reporting and safety inspection follow-up.
